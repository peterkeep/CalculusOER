Exercise 4.

For each of the solids described below, set up an integral using the disk/washer method that describes the volume of the solid. It will be helpful to visualize the region, a rectangle on that region, as well as the rectangle revolved around the axis of revolution.
🔗

(a)

The region bounded by the curve \(y=2x\) and the lines \(y=0\) and \(x=3\text{,}\) revolved around the \(x\)-axis.
🔗
Hint.

🔗
Solution.
\begin{align*} V \amp = \pi\int_{x=0}^{x=3} (2x)^2\;dx\\ \amp = \pi\int_{x=0}^{x=3} 4x^2\;dx\\ \amp = 36\pi \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

The region bounded by the curve \(y=e^{-2x}\) and the \(x\)-axis between \(x=0\) and \(x=\ln(2)\text{,}\) revolved around the \(x\)-axis.
🔗
Hint.

🔗
Solution.
\begin{align*} V \amp = \pi \int_{x=0}^{x=\ln(2)} (e^{-2x})^2\;dx\\ \amp = \pi \int_{x=0}^{x=\ln(2)} e^{-4x}\;dx\\ \amp = \frac{15\pi}{64} \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
🔗

(c)

The region bounded by the curves \(y=\ln(x)\) and \(y=\sqrt{x}\) between \(y=0\) and \(y=1\text{,}\) revolved around the \(y\)-axis.
🔗
Hint.

🔗
Solution.
\begin{align*} V \amp = \pi \int_{y=0}^{y=1} (e^y)^2 - (y^2)^2\;dy\\ \amp = \pi \int_{y=0}^{y=1} e^{2y} - y^4\;dy\\ \amp \pi\left(\frac{e^2}{2}-\frac{7}{10}\right) \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
🔗

(d)

The region bounded by the curves \(y=2x+1\) and \(y=x\) between \(x=0\) and \(x=3\text{,}\) revolved around the \(x\)-axis.
🔗
Hint.

🔗
Solution.
\begin{align*} V \amp = \pi \int_{x=0}^{x=3} (2x+1)^2 - x^2\;dx\\ \amp = \pi \int_{x=0}^{x=3} 3x^2+4x+1\;dx\\ \amp 48 \pi \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
🔗

(e)

The region bounded by the curve \(y=x^3\text{,}\) the \(x\)-axis, and the line \(x=2\text{,}\) revolved around the \(y\)-axis.
🔗
Hint.

🔗
Solution.
\begin{align*} V \amp = \pi \int_{y=0}^{y=8} (2)^2 - (\sqrt[3]{y})^2\;dy\\ \amp = \pi \int_{y=0}^{y=8} 4 - y^{2/3}\;dy\\ \amp = \frac{64\pi}{5} \end{align*}
🔗
🔗
🔗

(f)

The region bounded by the curve \(y=x^3\) and the \(y\)-axis between \(y=0\) and \(y=2\text{,}\) revolved around the \(y\)-axis.
🔗
Hint.

🔗
Solution.
\begin{align*} V \amp = \pi \int_{y=0}^{y=2} (\sqrt[3]{y})^2\;dy\\ \amp = \pi \int_{y=0}^{y=2} y^{2/3}\;dy\\ \amp = \frac{6\pi 2{2/3}}{5} \end{align*}
